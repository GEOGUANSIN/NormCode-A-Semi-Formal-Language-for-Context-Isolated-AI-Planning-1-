\begin{abstract}
Practitioners building AI agent workflows face a fragmented landscape: raw LLMs require adapting to each model's prompt style and quirks, code frameworks like LangChain demand programming expertise that limits who can read or maintain them, and no-code orchestration UIs cannot scale to complex multi-step scenarios. What is missing is a \textit{working language}---a shared, portable representation that any stakeholder can author, verify, and reuse across models, frameworks, and roles. Like SQL for databases, a language for AI agent workflows can be learned once and applied everywhere, with plans that are readable, version-controlled, and executable by any compliant orchestrator.

We present \textbf{NormCode}, a semi-formal language and execution framework that fills this role. At its core, NormCode enforces \textbf{data isolation}: each inference step operates on only explicitly passed inputs, eliminating \textit{context pollution}---the accumulation of information across reasoning steps that causes models to hallucinate and lose track of constraints. This makes AI plans \textit{auditable by construction}: for any step, you can determine exactly what it received, whether its operation was probabilistic or deterministic, and what it produced---properties increasingly demanded by regulated domains (legal, medical, financial) and by emerging legislation such as the EU AI Act.

The ecosystem supports three roles: \textit{Agent Designers} author and compile plans through a four-phase pipeline (Derivation, Formalization, Post-Formalization, Activation); \textit{Product Operators} inspect, debug, and manage plans through a visual Canvas App with real-time graph visualization and breakpoint debugging; and \textit{End Users} interact through purpose-built client interfaces. The multi-format design (\texttt{.ncds} for authoring, \texttt{.ncd} for execution, \texttt{.ncn} for human verification) allows each stakeholder to engage with the same underlying plan in a format suited to their needs. We validate the approach through 100\% accuracy on base-X addition and self-hosted execution of NormCode's own compiler pipeline, demonstrating that structured intermediate representations can bridge human intuition and machine rigor while maintaining full transparency.
\end{abstract}
