\section{Background}
\label{sec:background}

\subsection{The CBR Cycle}

Case-Based Reasoning \cite{aamodt1994,kolodner1993} solves new problems by retrieving
and adapting solutions from a case base of past experiences. The standard four-stage
cycle --- \textbf{Retrieve} a similar past case, \textbf{Reuse} its solution as a
starting point, \textbf{Revise} it to fit the new problem, \textbf{Retain} the result
as a new case --- provides a principled framework for experience-based problem solving.

The quality of the CBR cycle depends critically on the integrity of cases: a case must
be a self-contained, reliable record of a past solution state. Cases contaminated with
irrelevant or implicit context produce unreliable retrieval and incorrect reuse.

\subsection{Process-Oriented CBR}

Traditional CBR treats cases as problem--solution pairs \cite{kolodner1993}.
Process-Oriented CBR (POCBR) extends this to \textbf{processes as cases} --- the case
captures a workflow trace: the sequence of operations, their intermediate states, and
the data that flowed between steps \cite{bergmann2002,minor2014}.

POCBR is valuable for complex multi-step tasks where the path to a solution matters as
much as the solution itself. Applications include business process management,
scientific workflow reuse, and clinical pathway adaptation.

Multi-step LLM workflows are a natural POCBR domain: they are complex processes whose
intermediate states contain valuable information about what worked, what failed, and
where intervention was needed. The challenge is ensuring that intermediate states are
suitable CBR cases.

\subsection{The Case Integrity Problem in LLM Workflows}

The fundamental requirement for POCBR is \textbf{case integrity}: each intermediate
state in a workflow trace must be self-contained --- its stored data must be exactly
what the next step needs, with no implicit dependency on information that is not stored
in the case itself.

Most LLM orchestration frameworks do not satisfy this requirement by default. A
checkpoint captured at step $k$ in a typical orchestration framework is not a clean
case: it is a snapshot of shared state at that moment, which may embed accumulated
conversation history or prompt context from steps $1, \ldots, k-1$. Three consequences
follow:

\textbf{Unreliable retrieval.} Retrieving such a checkpoint and continuing from it in a
new execution context produces incorrect behavior: the implicit context the checkpoint
depended on is no longer present.

\textbf{Non-localizable failures.} When step $k$ fails, the cause may be anywhere in
the accumulated implicit state. Revision requires locating this cause --- which is not
possible when the context boundary is implicit.

\textbf{Irreproducible cases.} Two executions that load the same checkpoint may produce
different outputs because the implicit context that was present during the original
execution is not present during reuse.

These three failures are structural consequences of the absence of an isolation
guarantee.

\subsection{Structural Isolation as the Enabling Condition}

POCBR in LLM workflows requires that intermediate execution states are
case-integrity-preserving --- a property that must be guaranteed at the level of the
workflow language, not inferred post-hoc from logs.

\textbf{Definition.} A workflow language provides \textit{structural isolation} if:
(1)~each step can access only data explicitly declared in its own scope block;
(2)~the compiler determines each step's scope during formalization; (3)~the orchestrator
constructs each step's input from only its declared references, enforcing the constraint
at runtime by construction --- a step simply never receives undeclared data.

If a language provides structural isolation, then for every completed step $k$: the
stored output tensor contains exactly the data downstream steps will need; any failure
at step $k+1$ has a cause in the declared inputs of $k+1$; retrieving the checkpoint at
step $k$ and continuing from it in a new context will produce the same
data-dependency behavior as the original execution.

NormCode \cite{anon2025} is designed around this enabling condition.
